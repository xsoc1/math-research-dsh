# Turn proof attempts into reusable understanding

Keep a note when it changes what a later researcher can do. The library plugin
can index a small card containing the operation or insight, conditions, and a
pointer to the proof, program or original source. Free-form explanation is often
more useful than another status field.

A useful failed-route note explains a particular obstruction. For example:
replacing a trigonometric constraint by a coarse bound may discard the phase
information needed for a sign estimate. This diagnoses that relaxation on its
tested domain. It does not disprove the original sign statement. A sharper phase
bound is a concrete reason to revisit the route. A job that produced no response
has no mathematical failure mechanism to infer.

A successful route can teach more than its final theorem: a hypothesis may be
unused for one intermediate conclusion, a coordinate change may reveal a common
structure, or an exact rational certificate may replace repeated numerical work.
Record the precise conclusion for which an assumption was unnecessary. Do not
remove it from a larger theorem without checking the larger argument.

Compare routes when the comparison can distinguish ideas. Examples:

- Do different proofs use the same invariant in different coordinates?
- Is a failed bound repaired by information already retained by another route?
- Does a construction work on a new boundary case predicted by the explanation?

Write the proposed explanation and one way to falsify or discriminate it. Keep
the explanation a candidate until the mathematical work supports it. Automated
comparison can assemble shared assumptions and evidence pointers; it cannot
certify a new invariant merely by finding matching words.

The project understanding page is a place to discuss these ideas with the human
collaborator. Distinguish current explanations, competing hypotheses, changed
beliefs and open decisions. Preserve corrections and intuition in the user's own
words where helpful. Retrieve only the relevant underlying material when the
next problem arises; a longer library is not automatically a better one.
